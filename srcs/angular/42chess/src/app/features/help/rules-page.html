How games end

Games follow the FIDE Laws of Chess. The server checks every move and decides every result: what your browser shows is only a view of the game.

Winning

  • Checkmate: the king is attacked and has no legal way out.
  • Resignation: a player gives up with the Resign button.
  • Time: a player whose clock reaches zero loses, unless the opponent has no way left to checkmate (a lone king, or a king and a single bishop or knight against a lone king): the game is then drawn.
  • Abandonment: when your opponent leaves the game, you are told at once. If they have not come back after one minute, you can claim the win, or keep waiting.

Draws

  • Stalemate: the player to move has no legal move and is not in check.
  • Insufficient material: neither side can checkmate any more, for example king against king.
  • Agreement: a player offers a draw and the opponent accepts it. After a refused offer, the same player plays two more moves before offering again.
  • Claim: the player to move can claim a draw when the same position appeared three times, or after fifty moves by each side without a capture or a pawn move. A Claim draw button appears in the game when it applies.
  • Automatic: without any claim, the game is drawn when the same position appears five times, or after seventy-five moves by each side without a capture or a pawn move.

Aborted games

Before both players have played their first move, either player can abort the game. Between two people, a player who does not play their first move within 30 seconds aborts it too. An aborted game has no result, changes no rating and does not appear in the history.

Rated and casual games

  • Find an opponent always gives a rated game against a player of similar Elo. The colours are drawn at random.
  • Play a friend gives a casual game, unless you tick Rated game before creating it. An invitation nobody accepts is removed after 24 hours, and you can cancel it from the Play page.
  • Games against the computer are never rated.

A rated game changes both Elo ratings: you gain more for beating a stronger player than a weaker one. A player plays one game against another person at a time.

Time controls

Each player has a clock. The first number is the time for the whole game in minutes, the second the seconds added after each of your moves.
